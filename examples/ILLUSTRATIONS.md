# Illustration guidance

Use visuals when they explain a relationship, comparison or process more clearly than prose. An essay may need no visuals. The preferences below apply only when a visual is requested or agreed; they do not require a cover image or an illustration for every section.

## Style block

For conceptual illustrations, use fine pen-and-ink linework with crosshatching and stippling. Build the composition around one clear idea. Use a simple arrangement with enough open space for the subject to remain legible at the size it will appear in the essay.

Use black ink and one restrained terracotta accent on an evenly toned warm cream background. Reserve the accent for the element that carries the main point. Avoid photorealism, gradients, drop shadows, glows, simulated paper damage and three-dimensional rendering effects. Do not include logos, signatures or decorative lettering. Add a short label only when it is necessary to understand the image.

For diagrams and charts, use clean vector lines, consistent shapes and readable labels rather than decorative crosshatching. Preserve the actual relationships, direction of flow and data. Use the same restrained palette where it remains accessible. Do not distort quantities or introduce unsupported connections to make a composition more attractive.

## Aspect

- Wide conceptual illustration, if requested: prefer `16:9`.
- Small inline illustration, if requested: prefer `1:1`.
- Diagrams and charts: choose the aspect ratio that preserves readable labels and clear relationships. Split an overcrowded figure rather than shrinking the text.
- The intended reading surface may override these preferences. Check legibility at the final display or print size.

## Accuracy and accessibility

- Use labels, line styles or shapes as well as color to distinguish meaningful categories.
- Keep essential labels readable and high contrast. Avoid text embedded in generated artwork when a separately typeset label would be clearer.
- Alt text states the visual's purpose and the relationship it shows. A caption adds context, source information or limitations; it does not merely repeat the alt text.
- Identify illustrative or hypothetical data explicitly. Use sourced values for factual charts.
- Keep final assets and editable diagram or chart sources with the essay. Keep candidate images and iteration notes in the configured cache.
