# Audience

A sample reader profile for essays that explain systems, processes and practical decisions. Use alongside [VOICE.md](VOICE.md), adapting the assumptions to the intended publication.

## The reader

The reader helps plan or improve work in a small or medium-sized team. They understand the basics of schedules, budgets, handoffs and accountability, but may not know the specialist terminology of the subject being discussed.

They are reading to understand why something happens, compare plausible approaches or decide what to investigate next. They value a clear explanation of how a proposal works, what it costs and when it may fail. They are skeptical of unsupported certainty and broad promises.

Do not assume a particular employer, industry, country, software stack or job title. Explain technical details to the level needed for the decision. The reader should leave able to describe the mechanism, recognize its limits and identify a useful next question.

## Register

Write as an informed colleague explaining a subject to another capable adult. Be direct and respectful. Define unfamiliar terms without apologizing for the explanation or implying that the reader should already know them. Avoid sales language and insider shorthand.

## Loosen for the reader (density)

- Introduce one unfamiliar mechanism at a time. Show its inputs, what happens and the outcome.
- Use a short hypothetical example when a concept is difficult to picture. Make the assumption and the example's limits explicit.
- Explain why a technical detail matters before adding implementation detail.
- Keep essential qualifications in the main text. Put optional background in a note when the essay remains understandable without it.
- Define acronyms at first use unless they are ordinary language for this specific audience.

## De-jargon table

Apply a replacement only when it preserves the intended meaning and fits the sentence. Keep a technical term when precision requires it, and explain it on first use instead.

| Insider term | Plain replacement |
| :-- | :-- |
| utilize | use |
| prior to | before |
| subsequent to | after |
| in order to | to |
| at this point in time | now |

## Figure text (accessibility vs. print)

Alt text should communicate the purpose and main relationship shown by the figure. For a process diagram, state the important sequence or branch. For a chart, state the comparison and main pattern; provide underlying values in accessible text or a table when readers need them.

Captions add context, source information and relevant limitations. They should be useful in print and should not merely repeat the alt text. Essential meaning must not depend on color alone. Keep titles short; do not put information needed to understand the essay only in a hover title. Optional visual style preferences live in [ILLUSTRATIONS.md](ILLUSTRATIONS.md).
